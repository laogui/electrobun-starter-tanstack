## Design Context

### Users

GhostBoard serves visual thinkers—designers, researchers, writers, students,
and other people who organize mixed notes, images, links, and files on a large
spatial canvas. They need to understand collections at a glance and move
between overview and detail without losing their mental map.

### Brand Personality

Calm, tactile, and spatial. Interactions should feel like handling lightweight
paper objects on a desk: direct and expressive, but never bouncy, ornamental,
or visually noisy.

### Aesthetic Direction

Use an airy light canvas, softly tinted paper surfaces, restrained shadows, and
physical folder/card metaphors. Folder expansion should resemble a controlled
bottom-up waterfall of heterogeneous cards. Preserve natural card proportions
and stable object identity across every transition. Dark mode should keep the
same hierarchy and material relationships rather than introducing glow effects.

### Design Principles

1. Stable identity over novelty: the same card must remain recognizable before,
   during, and after a transition.
2. Reversible spatial motion: opening and closing follow the same captured
   paths in opposite directions.
3. Keep the user's camera and mental map intact; local interactions must not
   unexpectedly pan or zoom the canvas.
4. Randomness is deterministic and chosen once per collection state, never
   recalculated during an animation.
5. Animate only transforms and opacity, use restrained exponential easing, and
   provide a reduced-motion equivalent.
